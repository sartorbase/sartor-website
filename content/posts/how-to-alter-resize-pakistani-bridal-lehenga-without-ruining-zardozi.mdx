## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why do Western dry-cleaners and prom tailors ruin hand-embroidered Pakistani bridal lehengas?
Western bridal tailors work with lace appliques and polyester satin, not coiled metallic dabka and hollow kora wired across 24 radial silk kalis. Running a rotary cutter or overlocker over zardozi bullion shatters sewing needles, slices the holding silk cotton thread beneath copper coils, and triggers an irreversible domino unraveling across the entire panel. Master ateliers unpick individual kali seams by hand and re-anchor cut metallic endpoints safely before sewing.

### How do you lengthen or shorten a heavily embroidered Pakistani bridal lehenga without chopping the bottom border?
You never cut straight across the bottom daman border. To shorten a finished lehenga safely, Master Tailor Abdul Ghaffar detaches the upper waistband and adjusts height strictly from the top pelvic line—or introduces an invisible micro-tuck above the lower scalloped border. To lengthen a short heirloom lehenga by 1.5 to 3 inches, we install a concealed color-matched 100 GSM Katan silk yoke beneath the choli line or a hand-finished silk velvet hem facing.

### Can a vintage or mother's heirloom bridal lehenga get resized cleanly with modern corsetry comfort?
Yes. Heirloom 1990s or 2000s lehengas frequently suffer from thin fraying drawstrings and loose boxy cholis. SARTOR strips decaying cotton linings, stabilizes vintage silk seams with breathable 60 GSM Habotai silk backing, upgrades the waist onto a 3-tier 280 GSM cotton canvas suspension band with brass hook bars, and drafts a fresh princess-seamed velvet or Katan silk choli matched precisely to vintage bullion patina.

### What if a boutique lehenga choli is 2 inches too tight across the bust or pinching under the arms?
When commercial cholis lack hidden reserves, forcing the zipper splits silk along the bullion edge. SARTOR checks internal allowance reserves first; if none remain, we insert sculpted side-underarm gussets in matching 100 GSM Katan silk (or deep garnet velvet) paired with a deepened anterior scye curve and German Rigilene side stays—keeping 1.5 to 2.0-inch internal Habotai-bound margins for future security.
