## 2. Shoulder Slope & High-Armhole *Scye* Geometry for *Baraat* Mobility

On his *Baraat* day, a Pakistani groom is constantly moving: greeting dozens of elders in *milni* embraces, lifting arms for *sehra bandi*, sitting cross-legged or on low gilded diwans, and celebrating during entrance dances.

Off-the-rack sizing blocks assume identical generic shoulder slopes and slash oversized, drooping armholes (*deep scyes*) so thicker arms squeeze inside without custom pattern grading. Paradoxically, a *bigger, lower* armhole restricts movement—acting like a leash that pulls the entire coat collar and hemline upward the moment you raise an elbow.

### SARTOR's Bilateral Shoulder & Scye Engineering

Through 13+ years of precision cutting under Master Tailor Abdul Ghaffar, SARTOR solves upper-body *Baraat* stiffness with three pattern interventions:

1. **Asymmetrical Shoulder Slope Calibration:** Almost every athletic or office-working groom carries a 0.25-to-0.5-inch drop on his dominant shoulder. Rather than stuffing thick foam pads to mask posture, we grade left and right shoulder seams independently so both collar points rest symmetrically.
2. **High-Scye Rotational Sleeve Head:** We draft the underarm *scye* base high—roughly 0.75 inches below your natural armpit—and pair it with a sculpted forward sleeve-head roll (*khund*), enabling 180-degree arm elevation with zero chest distortion.
3. **Contoured Two-Piece Coat Back & Waist Suppression:** We shape the center-back seam along your spinal S-curve while preserving **1.5 to 2.0 inches** of finished internal seam margins inside the back and side-body seams for future weight shifts.

## 3. Matching Woven Banarsi Jamawar Motifs Across Front Plackets

Nothing signals amateur assembly faster on an heirloom **Banarsi Jamawar sherwani** than broken floral medallions chopped in half down the front opening.

Handloom *Kadhwa* Banarsi Jamawar features complex repeating *paisley*, *gul-e-daudi*, or architectural *jaal* motifs woven directly into the Katan silk warp. Because standard cutting rooms stack fabric sheets haphazardly to minimize cloth waste, left and right front panels rarely align symmetrically across the center button line.

### The SARTOR Book-Matching & Grain Alignment Rules

* **Optical Motif Indexing Before Chalk Marking:** Master Tailor Abdul Ghaffar lays out full 3.5-meter Jamawar bolts across our cutting table to map repeat spacing first—allocating extra yardage solely to align identical floral motifs horizontally across the chest, waist pockets, and concealed front placket.
* **Single-Vector Nap & Grain Plumb:** Even subtle directional sheen shifts between warp and weft make mismatched panels photograph slightly two-toned under flash. All panels, sleeves, and collar stands cut along strict vertical plumb grainlines.
* **Hand-Basted Overlap Concealed Placket (*Ghum Patti*):** We construct the 1.25-inch button overlap so every antique gold weave pattern flows continuously across the front closure as though carved from one unbroken panel—accented with hand-wrapped silk shank buttons or heirloom *polki-kundan* jewel studs.
* **60 GSM Pure Habotai Silk Lining + Internal Pocket Vault:** Backside *zari* threads on woven Jamawar snag silk kurtas underneath. We line the entire coat interior in cool, low-friction Habotai silk with reinforced concealed wallet and watch clearance.
