## 1. Fabric Verification: Demanding Pure Textures Over Poly-Blends

The cornerstone of authentic Pakistani couture lies in raw tactile weight. In unverified markets, synthetic polyester blends are routinely passed off as pure katan silk or genuine organza. Under bright wedding hall chandeliers and flash photography, polyester creates a harsh artificial sheen, traps perspiration, and puckers under heavy metallic embroidery.

When commissioning bespoke couture through SARTOR's custom bridal atelier, every ensemble starts with laboratory-verified, pure-grade textiles:

1. **Pure Rawa & Katan Silk (80–100 GSM)**: Sourced for structured lehengas, peshwas bodices, and cigarette trousers. Its tightly twisted warp offers the tensile strength required to anchor 8 to 12 kilograms of metallic wire without sagging.
2. **Pure Silk Organza & French Tissue**: Boasts an iridescent, multi-tonal metallic reflection woven with authentic gold or silver tilla threads in the weft, creating ethereal dupattas that drape weightlessly.
3. **Pure Banarsi Katan Jamawar**: Hand-loomed in traditional jacquard pits with heritage floral bootis and kadhwa motifs, forming an antique foundation for regal Walima ensembles.

> **Artisan Note:** Authentic hand zardozi requires pure silk fabrics with a minimum thread count of 120x100 threads per inch. Synthetic polyesters melt under friction during continuous needlework and cannot withstand the weight of genuine kora dabka.

## 2. Authentic Lahore Zardozi: Separating Karigari from Machine Foil

Lahore's historical embroidery lineage dates back to the Mughal Imperial ateliers of the 16th century, centered around the Walled City (Androon Lahore). True heirloom embroidery is executed by generational artisans (*ustads* and *karigars*) seated cross-legged at horizontal wooden stretching frames known as **addas**.

When reviewing custom bridal samples, brides must distinguish between hand-forged metallic work and mass-produced commercial substitutes:

* **Zardozi & Kora Dabka**: Hand-coiled French bullion wires cut into microscopic bugles and sewn individually with micro-needles to create three-dimensional relief.
* **Marori Work**: Golden metallic cord looped continuously into intricate arabesque borders, tacked down millimeter by millimeter using fine cotton thread.
* **Pure Silk Floss Resham**: Untwisted silk filaments dyed in bespoke pantone shades to introduce delicate ombre feathering into floral petals.
* **Mukesh & Kaamdani**: Pure metallic flat wire painstakingly pierced through delicate chiffon or net, folded, and burnished with a cowrie shell.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How many months in advance should an overseas bride place her custom order?
We strongly recommend booking your bridal ensemble **3 to 5 months** prior to your wedding date. This accommodates 8 to 12 weeks of meticulous hand embroidery on the adda, 2 weeks of master pattern cutting and finishing, and 5 business days for DHL Express delivery, leaving you ample time for personal accessorizing.

### What happens if my body weight changes before the wedding?
Every custom couture garment from SARTOR is crafted with built-in **2.5-inch finished seam allowances** inside the side seams, armholes, and lehenga waistbands. This permits up to two dress sizes of expansion or reduction through simple side seam adjustments that do not compromise the handwork.

Yes. Our master sketch artists can translate high-res inspiration photographs, mood boards, or vintage family heirlooms into custom hand-drafted patterns while adapting color palettes and neckline silhouettes to your personal modesty preferences.

### What if the adda embroidery swatch does not match my expectations?

### How are payments handled safely across international borders?
International clients can complete milestone payments using secure bank-to-bank international wires (IBAN/SWIFT) or major credit cards. You only release funds incrementally as each production milestone is validated through video proof.
