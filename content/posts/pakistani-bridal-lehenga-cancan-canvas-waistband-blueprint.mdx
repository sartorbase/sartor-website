## The Can-Can Crisis: The "Cheese-Grater" Mesh vs. SARTOR Modular Tiering

In commercial bridal salons across London's Southall or Toronto's Gerrard Street, lehengas are outfitted with cheap, mass-produced industrial nylon crinoline (*can-can*).

This crude shortcut creates four disastrous problems for diaspora brides:

### 1. Severe Thigh Chafing & Skin Lacerations
Bazaar can-can uses hard, sharp-edged 100% plastic mesh. The cut edges of this mesh are frequently left raw against cheap synthetic lining. Within an hour of walking, the sharp plastic fibers poke through the lining like tiny needles, scratching the bride's bare skin and ripping nylon hosiery.

### 2. Irreversible Packing Crushes
When a bride unpacks her wedding gown in London or New York after international shipping, she finds the stitched-in plastic netting crushed into flat, accordion-like wrinkles. Because plastic mesh has a memory, hanging or hand-steaming cannot restore its three-dimensional fullness, resulting in an asymmetrical, misshapen skirt on her wedding day.

### 3. The Rigid "Doll-Dress" Immobility
Stitched-in can-can forces the skirt into an unyielding cone. Sitting down on a bridal stage sofa causes the front hemline to fly upward awkwardly, exposing the bride's ankles and shoes while creating a massive wall of mesh between her and her groom.

### 4. Zero Post-Wedding Reusability
Once the wedding is over, an overseas bride can never wear her bridal lehenga again to a sibling's or close relative's formal wedding because the massive, built-in can-can dome looks excessively exaggerated.
