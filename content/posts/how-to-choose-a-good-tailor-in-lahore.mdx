## Where Tailoring in Lahore Usually Goes Wrong

When a custom outfit disappoints, the error rarely happens during sewing. It happens during cutting and client intake. Here are the five root causes of fitting failures in Lahore:

1. **The Restricted Armhole (*Munda*) and Tight Back Shoulders**: Cheap tailoring shops use generic one-size cardboard armhole templates. If the cutter does not account for bust fullness or shoulder slope, the sleeve binds whenever you reach forward or drive.
2. **Missing Seam Allowances (The Zero-Margin Trap)**: Many commercial tailors overlock and shave off excess fabric inside the side seams. If you gain two pounds, the garment is permanently ruined because there is zero fabric inside to let out.
3. **Ignoring the Fabric Grainline (*Kaan Nikalna*)**: When printed lawn or chiffon is rolled at the textile mill, the grainline often stretches askew. An inexperienced tailor simply cuts along the print, causing the kurti hem to twist toward your hip after the first wash.
4. **Mishandling Luxury Laces and Organza Extensions**: High-end Pakistani designer suits (Maria.B, Sana Safinaz, Sapphire, Bareeze) come with multiple embroidered patches and organza borders. Amateurs sew them flat with tight tension, causing unsightly wrinkles and fabric bunching.
5. **The Chronic Delivery Nightmare (*"Kal Ana"*)**: Bazaar tailors routinely accept double their workshop capacity during Eid and wedding seasons. The result is rushed, late-night stitching, careless finishing, and frantic excuses hours before your event.

## The 4 Physical Tests to Evaluate Any Tailor in Lahore

Before handing over expensive pure silk, festive velvet, or heavy formalwear, take one finished suit from the tailor's rack—or give them a single inexpensive everyday lawn suit to test. Inspect these four specific checkpoints:

### 1. The Turn-It-Inside-Out Test (Finishing & Seam Allowances)
Turn the garment completely inside out. The interior of a suit reveals the true standard of craftsmanship:
* **Check the side seams**: There must be at least **1.5 to 2.0 inches of finished fabric allowance** along both side seams and the sleeves.
* **Inspect the serging**: Overlock stitching should be clean, tight, and color-matched—never loose, tangled, or unraveling at the hem splits (*chaak*).
* **Look at the hem finish**: High-end tailoring uses concealed blind-hemming (*turpai*) or clean micro-piping (*maghzi*), rather than crude, visible machine topstitching.

### 2. The Neckline Flatness Test (Interfacing & Collarbone Balance)
Lay the shirt flat on a table:
* The front neckline must lie completely flat without bubbling, rippling, or gaping outward.
* Press the collar interfacing between your fingers. It should feel pliable and soft against the chest, not stiff like cardboard.
* The back neck facing must be cleanly secured with micro-stitches so it never flips outward when you move.

### 3. The Lace and Border Alignment Test
If the suit features separate embroidered organza patches or lace inserts:
* The join between the base lawn and the organza patch must be perfectly smooth without puckered gathers.
* Hemlines must hang completely parallel to the floor—measure the front and back lengths to ensure they match down to the quarter-inch.
* Side slits (*chaak*) must be reinforced at the top apex with a bar-tack stitch to prevent fabric tears when sitting down.

### 4. The Trouser Rise and Inseam Balance
Examine the trouser or shalwar:
* The back crotch rise must be cut 1.5 to 2 inches higher than the front rise. This ensures the trouser does not pull down when you sit on a sofa or in a car.
* The trouser opening (*paincha*) must have crisp, structured fusing that stays straight around the ankle without twisting inward.

## The Customer Preparation Checklist

The best tailors deliver their finest work when the customer provides clear, organized instructions. Follow this 6-step checklist before handing over your fabric:

* [ ] **Pre-shrink (*shrank*) all cottons and lawns**: Always soak unstitched cotton and lawn in cold water for 30 minutes and dry thoroughly before giving it to your tailor. Unshrunk lawn can shrink up to 1.5 inches after the first wash, ruining a fitted cut.
* [ ] **Count and bundle all unstitched pieces**: Check your designer suit box. Confirm that front panels, back panels, sleeves, embroidered neckline patches, hem borders, and trouser fabrics are all accounted for.
* [ ] **Specify measurements in numbers, not adjectives**: Instead of saying *"make the shirt a little loose"*, give exact specifications (e.g., *"chest 20.5 inches, waist 18 inches, shirt length 43 inches"*).
* [ ] **Decide on lining (*astar*) fabric**: For sheer chiffon or net fabrics, specify breathable pure lawn or cotton silk lining. Avoid synthetic polyester linings that trap heat and create static cling.
* [ ] **Confirm your preferred trouser cut**: State clearly whether you want straight cigarette trousers, culottes, classic shalwar, or tulip pants, and specify the ankle opening width in inches (e.g., *"6.5-inch paincha"*).
* [ ] **Demand a written receipt with a guaranteed date**: Ensure your receipt records the stitch rate, any extra costs for laces or lining, and the exact delivery date.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if a tailor understands my size if I send a sample suit?
When sending a sample garment (*naap*), verify that it currently fits you comfortably across the chest, shoulders, and hips. If you want any adjustments (such as adding 1 inch to the length or tapering the trouser), write these down clearly on a note attached to the sample. A reputable tailor will confirm those adjustments with you before cutting.

### What is the difference between simple suit stitching and double suit stitching?
A simple suit is constructed from opaque fabrics (like lawn, linen, or khaddar) that do not require inner backing. A double suit is crafted from sheer or delicate fabrics (such as chiffon, organza, net, or pure silk) and requires a full inner slip lining (*astar*) carefully basted and stitched into the garment to prevent transparency and add structural weight.
