## 1. Pure 120 GSM Katan Silk Base Physics: Tensile Load Resistance

The fundamental failure point of commercial bridalwear purchased from high-street bridal showrooms in London, Southall, or Toronto is fabric under-specification. Commercial retailers frequently cut bridal skirts on lightweight 70 to 80 GSM silk blends or cheap polyester raw silks to reduce manufacturing costs.

When four to six kilograms of metallic bullion wire are mounted onto a wooden embroidery frame (*adda*), the fabric is stretched under drum-tight tension with perimeter lacing cords. Every downward puncture of the hooked needle (*aar*) cuts into delicate yarns.

```
LIGHTWEIGHT COMMERCIAL SILK (70–80 GSM) UNDER 5KG EMBROIDERY:
[Fine Weft] -----|x|----- [Fine Warp]  --> Needle shears thin fibers
Result: Micro-perforations propagate into visible tears; skirt sags 3 inches.

SARTOR 120 GSM PURE KATAN MULBERRY SILK:
[High-Twist Warp] ======(·)====== [High-Twist Weft]
Result: Fibers part elastically around needle shaft; self-heals and locks bullion.
```

Pure Katan silk is woven from high-twist, double-filament Grade-6A mulberry silk yarns in both the warp and weft directions:
1. **Elastic Tensile Recovery:** When an artisan drives an *aar* needle through 120 GSM Katan silk to secure a coiled *dabka* spring or heavy *kora* bullion wire, the densely twisted natural filaments part cleanly without breaking. Once the needle withdraws, the silk fibers contract back around the thread anchor.
2. **Zero Bias Droop:** Heavier 120 GSM Katan silk possesses structural body that resists gravitational elongation. The panels retain their architectural flare throughout ten hours of standing, stage photography, and banquet dining.
3. **Generational Archival Durability:** Pure mulberry silk is a natural protein fiber that breathes. When preserved inside breathable, acid-free cotton muslin, a SARTOR Katan silk bridal lehenga will remain supple and free of fiber brittleness for thirty to fifty years, transforming into an enduring family heirloom.

## 2. Three-Dimensional Bullion Zardozi Engineering: Hand vs. Machine

For overseas brides commissioning couture from 6,000 miles away, identifying authentic hand craftsmanship is critical. Commercial bridal markets are flooded with garments claiming to be "pure handcrafted zardozi" that are actually computerized machine embroideries embellished with a few hand-glued crystals.

At SARTOR, our master *karigars* execute generational hand needlecraft on horizontal wooden *adda* frames, working with five distinct metallic alloys:

```
THE 3D HAND-COUCHED ZARDOZI EMBROIDERY MATRIX:
[Layer 1: Foundation] Pure 120 GSM Crimson Katan Silk Base
       ||
[Layer 2: Padding]    Hand-Strung Cotton Thread Padding ("Vasli")
       ||
[Layer 3: Core Relief]Coiled Pure Copper Bullion ("Kora" & "Dabka")
       ||
[Layer 4: Detailing]  Fine Silver-Gilt Wire ("Tilla") & French Micro-Sequins
       ||
[Layer 5: Accents]    Hand-Set Seed Pearls & Cut-Glass Crystals ("Sitara")
```

1. **Vasli Relief Padding:** To create dramatic three-dimensional depth, our artisans first sew dense layers of hand-twisted cotton thread beneath the master stencil (*khaka*). The metallic bullion is then couched over this raised core, creating architectural relief that casts rich, velvety micro-shadows under evening ballroom chandeliers.
2. **Kora & Dabka Spring Coils:** Authentic hand needlework utilizes flexible, hollow metallic springs. *Dabka* is an ultra-fine, tightly wound smooth spring that reflects light with subtle liquid sheen, while *kora* features a crimped, faceted surface that sparkles with antique warmth.
3. **Machine Tilla vs. Authentic Hand Zardozi:** Computerized machine embroidery uses flat, high-sheen synthetic polyester thread that lies completely flat against the cloth. Under professional flash photography, machine work creates an unflattering plastic glare. Authentic hand-worked copper bullion absorbs and softens flash, giving the bride a regal, timeless glow in high-resolution wedding films.
