## 1. Why Western Boutiques Markup Pakistani Bridal Wear by 300% (And How Atelier-Direct Pricing Works)

To understand bridal retail economics, brides must look at the supply chain journey between an embroidery frame in Lahore and a retail display rack in Mayfair, Mississauga, or Sugar Land.


### The Breakdown of Hidden Boutique Markups

1. **Commercial Real Estate Leases:** Premium South Asian shopping districts—such as Southall Broadway in London, Oak Tree Road in Edison, New Jersey, or Gerrard Street in Toronto—command exorbitant commercial rents and local council tax rates. A boutique selling 10 to 15 bridal outfits a month must build $1,500 to $2,500 of showroom overhead into each individual invoice.
2. **Third-Party Agent Commissions:** Most Western boutique owners do not own workshops in Lahore. They work through Pakistani sourcing agents (*arhtis*), who add a 25% to 40% intermediary fee before garments even leave Pakistan.
3. **Rigid Commercial Block Sizing:** To minimize production costs, wholesale suppliers cut garments according to generic standard size blocks (UK 10, US 6). They shave internal seam allowances down to 0.5 inches to economize on raw silk yardage. If the bride gains or loses weight before her wedding, the dress cannot be let out.
4. **Currency Volatility & Hedging:** Boutiques add a 15% to 20% foreign currency risk cushion into Western retail prices to protect themselves against PKR-USD-GBP exchange rate swings over 6- to 9-month delivery cycles.
